## Read the passage

Read Psalm 23 aloud. Notice where David speaks *about* the LORD (“he”) and where he starts speaking *to* him (“you”).

## Observe

- What does the shepherd do? Make a list of the verbs.
- What does the sheep need? What dangers are mentioned?
- Where does the image change from a field to a table?

## Interpret

**A shepherd king.** David had been a shepherd before he was king (1 Samuel 16:11; 17:34–35). He knew that sheep cannot find water or pasture alone, and that a shepherd's job included fighting off wild animals. Calling the LORD *my shepherd* is not a soft image; it is a confession of complete dependence.

**From “he” to “you”.** In verses 1–3 David talks *about* God. In verse 4, in the darkest valley, he begins talking *to* him: *“for you are with me.”* The danger does not disappear, but it turns description into relationship.

> “Even though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil, for you are with me. Your rod and your staff, they comfort me.” (Psalm 23:4, WEB)

**Rod and staff.** The rod was a club for defending the flock; the staff was a long stick for guiding and pulling sheep back. Protection and correction both turn out to be comfort.

**Goodness that follows.** The word translated *follow* (Hebrew *radaph*) usually means *to pursue* or *chase*. God's goodness and loving kindness are not waiting somewhere ahead; they are running after us.

## Apply

Psalm 23 does not promise a life without valleys. It promises a Shepherd in them. Trust here is not the absence of fear but knowing who walks beside you.

## Questions for reflection or discussion

1. Which verb from your list do you need most right now?
2. Think of a “valley” you have walked through. How did you experience God's presence there, or his absence?
3. What would it look like this week to be *pursued* by God's goodness?

## Prayer

Lord, you are my shepherd. Lead me, restore me, and stay with me in every valley. Amen.
