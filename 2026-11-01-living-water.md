## Read the passage

Read John 4:1–26 slowly, preferably twice. The first time, just follow the story. The second time, notice every question that is asked, and by whom.

## Observe: what does the text say?

Jesus is travelling from Judea to Galilee, and John notes that he *“needed to pass through Samaria”* (v. 4). He arrives at Jacob's well at about noon, tired from the journey, and asks a Samaritan woman for a drink.

- Who starts the conversation, and how?
- How many times does the woman misunderstand what Jesus means?
- Where does the conversation turn from water to her own life?

## Interpret: below the surface

**A road most people avoided.** Many Jews travelling north took a longer route to avoid Samaria. The Greek word for *needed* (*edei*) is often used in John for what is necessary in God's plan. The route is not only geography; it is purpose.

**The sixth hour.** Women usually drew water in the cooler part of the day, together. Coming alone at noon may hint that she was avoiding others. Jesus meets her exactly where she tries to stay unseen.

**Two kinds of water.** Jesus speaks of *“living water”* (v. 10). In everyday speech this meant flowing, fresh water rather than water standing in a cistern. The woman hears plumbing; Jesus means life from God himself (compare Jeremiah 2:13).

> “Whoever drinks of the water that I will give him will never thirst again; but the water that I will give him will become in him a well of water springing up to eternal life.” (John 4:14, WEB)

**Truth without rejection.** When Jesus names her five husbands (v. 18), he does not walk away. He reveals what she has been hiding, and in the same conversation reveals who he is: *“I am he, the one who speaks to you”* (v. 26). It is the first clear *I am* in John's gospel, and it is spoken to an outsider.

## Apply: what does this mean for us?

The woman came for water and left her water jar behind (v. 28). What she found mattered more than what she came for. We often bring God our practical needs, and he cares about those, yet he also wants to reach the thirst beneath them.

## Questions for reflection or discussion

1. Where in your life are you going back to the same “well” again and again without being satisfied?
2. Jesus starts by asking for help rather than offering it. What does that teach us about reaching people?
3. Is there anything you tend to keep hidden from God? How does this story change the way you see being fully known?
4. Who are the “Samaritans” in your world, people you would normally avoid?

## Prayer

Lord Jesus, you know me completely and you still come to meet me. Give me the living water that only you can give, and help me to share it with others. Amen.
