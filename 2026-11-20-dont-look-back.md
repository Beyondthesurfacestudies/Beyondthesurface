*This study is made up of four short devotionals. Each one begins with the Bible passages to read first. Scripture quotations are from the World English Bible (WEB).*

## 1. Her Heart Was Still in Sodom

**Read:** Genesis 13:1–13 · Luke 17:22–33 · Matthew 6:21 · Acts 7:39

Lot's wife looked back. She had been given four instructions by the angel: flee for your life, don't look behind you, don't stop anywhere in the plain, and finally, escape to the mountains (Genesis 19:17).

It was chaos. Sulfur and fire were raining down from the sky, and the air was filled with smoke and terrifying sounds. There had been no time to say goodbye, no time to gather precious belongings. Time was running out.

Her heart was still in Sodom. She had built her life there. Her children had grown up there, and her husband held a high position in society. That city was her life, her whole existence. Everything she knew and loved was there.

And now, suddenly, her city was on fire, and everything was being destroyed. Lot's wife was torn apart inside. She couldn't let go. Would she really never be able to return to everything that was so familiar to her? She looked back, just one more time…

> But his wife looked back from behind him, and she became a pillar of salt. (Genesis 19:26)

The Hebrew word for “look” here is *nabat*. It means to look intently, with focus, to gaze at something. This was not a quick glance. God uses the same word in Genesis 15:5 when He tells Abraham: “Look now toward the sky, and count the stars.” Abraham looks up toward the promise; she looks back toward what is being lost. The same word also appears in Numbers 21:9: whoever looked at the bronze serpent lived.

Some 500 to 600 years later, the Israelites were having a hard time in the wilderness. They complained, plotted rebellions, and accused Moses and Aaron. Their hearts were full of discontent. They no longer thought about what God had done for them. The miraculous crossing through the sea faded into the background. Their deliverance from slavery grew dim in their memory. Instead, they thought about what they missed: good food, the things they knew, everything that felt familiar. They longed to go back to Egypt. They romanticized the good and forgot the bad.

> Our fathers wouldn't be obedient to him, but rejected him, and turned back in their hearts to Egypt. (Acts 7:39)

Just as the heart of Lot's wife was still in Sodom.

And that is the heart of the problem. Jesus says:

> “For where your treasure is, there your heart will be also.” (Matthew 6:21)

The thing you cherish deep inside and don't want to let go of is exactly the thing that keeps you captive. It holds you in a place that isn't good for you.

**Reflect:** Do you recognize this in your own life?

## 2. Guard Your Heart

**Read:** Proverbs 4:23

Lot's wife couldn't let go of Sodom. For Lot and his wife, and for the Israelites, it was something very big. But it can be something smaller, too. Something that distracts us again and again, something that pulls us away from God.

Compare it to a beautiful house with a rotting foundation. At first you don't see it. You fall in love with its charm, the beautiful woodwork, the big windows. You picture life in the spacious kitchen, all the potential of the rooms, cozy evenings in the comfortable living room, the people you'll welcome, the dinners you'll host. Meanwhile, the decay keeps eating away at the edges of the house. Until, one day, everything you were leaning on suddenly collapses. And then it's too late. Proverbs warns:

> Keep your heart with all diligence, for out of it is the wellspring of life. (Proverbs 4:23)

For the Israelites, what was gnawing deep in their hearts eventually came out. It showed itself in their actions. They began to put God to the test. In the end, they even went back to honoring the idols of Egypt: idols that had never helped them, but that were familiar, that reminded them of their old life in Egypt. In their hearts, they returned to their old existence in slavery. Spiritually, they were still captives. Strangers in a land they were never meant for. A land that would never truly be their home.

**Reflect:** Are there things in your life that quietly take over your heart and your thoughts, without you meaning them to?

## 3. Step by Step Toward Sodom

**Read:** Genesis 13:10–13 · Genesis 19:1–20 · 2 Peter 2:6–9

Do you remember how Abraham let Lot choose the land? Lot chose the most attractive land:

> Lot lifted up his eyes, and saw all the plain of the Jordan, that it was well-watered everywhere, before Yahweh destroyed Sodom and Gomorrah, like the garden of Yahweh, like the land of Egypt. (Genesis 13:10)

A fertile land, with a lively city and a spirit of trade. A place where Lot could become successful, where he could build his life and raise his children in an environment full of opportunities and comforts.

Did Lot know that something was fundamentally wrong? Absolutely. In 2 Peter 2 we read that he was “tormented in his righteous soul from day to day with seeing and hearing lawless deeds” (2 Peter 2:8). Day after day he saw their wicked deeds, and he must have suffered greatly because of it. And yet he stayed, for reasons we don't know. Maybe he saw no other way forward. Maybe he was too attached to everything he had there.

Either way, the torment in his soul didn't stop him from staying. It didn't even stop him from getting involved in the life of the city. Little by little, he moves closer. In Genesis 13:12 he pitches his tents as far as Sodom. In 14:12 he lives in Sodom. And in 19:1 he sits in the city gate, the place for influential people who had a say in the affairs of the city and its inhabitants.

Let's look back for a moment. In Genesis 18:32, Abraham pleaded with God to spare his nephew's city if just ten righteous people could be found there. And then, in Genesis 19:4, we read the chilling fact that all the men of Sodom, young and old, without exception, surrounded Lot's house:

> The men of the city, the men of Sodom, surrounded the house, both young and old, all the people from every quarter. (Genesis 19:4)

Can you imagine? If this happened today, it would be all over the news. This couldn't have been an isolated incident. It was a violent eruption of years of moral decay among the people of Sodom, the bitter fruit of the corrupt culture there. And this was the society in which Lot and his wife raised their children.

**Reflect:** Lot moved toward Sodom step by step. Is there something in your life that you have slowly grown toward, even though you know it isn't bringing you closer to God?

## 4. Remember Lot's Wife

**Read:** Genesis 19:12–26 · Luke 17:33 · Hebrews 11:15–16 · Philippians 3:13–14

Lot didn't belong there. In Genesis 19:9 the men of Sodom say:

> “This one fellow came in to live as a foreigner, and he appoints himself a judge.” (Genesis 19:9)

Even his own future sons-in-law don't take him seriously at all; to them he seems to be joking (19:14). It's as if Lot's choices had made him more and more spiritually blind. He even hesitates to flee and still tries to negotiate where he can go. But God wanted to spare him, and he was pulled out by the hand. It's a sad pile-up of wrong choices, and it costs them everything. Lot's wife turns into a pillar of salt.

Jesus Himself speaks about Lot's wife, as a clear warning: “Remember Lot's wife!” (Luke 17:32). She tried to hold on to her life, to everything she had built and had become attached to. Her heart was in Sodom. Jesus adds:

> “Whoever seeks to save his life loses it, but whoever loses his life preserves it.” (Luke 17:33)

This warning still speaks to us today. Knowingly or unknowingly, we may have built our lives on a foundation that isn't healthy. We may have come to feel at home in a place where we are really strangers.

That's why it is so important to dedicate our lives and our hearts to God. Not to look back or long for things that may be comfortable, but that don't bring us closer to Him.

I want to close with Hebrews 11. That chapter is beautiful, because it's about people who died in faith. They acknowledged that they were strangers and pilgrims on the earth (11:13).

> If indeed they had been thinking of that country from which they went out, they would have had enough time to return. But now they desire a better country, that is, a heavenly one. Therefore God is not ashamed of them, to be called their God, for he has prepared a city for them. (Hebrews 11:15–16)

They, too, faced the temptation to long for what they knew. But they longed for something better, something more beautiful: a heavenly homeland. That is what we were made for too! When we fix our focus, our gaze, on that, we are already living in God's will.

This world is far from perfect, and so are our lives. The difference between Lot's wife and these believers is not in what they lost, but in where their longing was directed. In the words of Paul:

> But one thing I do: forgetting the things which are behind, and stretching forward to the things which are before, I press on toward the goal for the prize of the high calling of God in Christ Jesus. (Philippians 3:13–14)
