# Word Studies

Sometimes one word in the original language puts a whole story in a new light. These are the Hebrew and Greek words we have explored in the studies so far, with a short explanation and a link to the study where you can read more.

## Hebrew

### dag / dagah
<span class="term-lang">Hebrew · fish</span>

In Jonah 1:17 the fish is masculine (*dag*), in Jonah 2:1 it is feminine (*dagah*). The inside of the fish becomes a kind of womb from which Jonah comes out as a new person.

<span class="term-study">Study: </span>[Jonah: Running from a Merciful God](/studies/the-book-of-jonah/)

### ereṣ
<span class="term-lang">Hebrew · earth, land</span>

Usually simply “earth” or “land,” but sometimes used for the realm of the dead, the place from which no one returns (Jonah 2:6; 1 Samuel 28:13).

<span class="term-study">Study: </span>[Jonah](/studies/the-book-of-jonah/)

### ganab
<span class="term-lang">Hebrew · to steal, to do in secret</span>

Eliphaz says a word was “secretly brought” to him (Job 4:12). The verb carries the sense of something stolen or sneaky.

<span class="term-study">Study: </span>[Job: Where Is God When Everything Falls Apart?](/studies/the-book-of-job/)

### go'el
<span class="term-lang">Hebrew · kinsman-redeemer</span>

The family member who had the legal duty to rescue a relative in trouble, like Boaz for Ruth. Job says: “I know that my Redeemer lives” (Job 19:25).

<span class="term-study">Study: </span>[Job](/studies/the-book-of-job/)

### ha-satan
<span class="term-lang">Hebrew · the accuser</span>

In Job 1 Satan is called *ha-satan*, “the accuser.” He is not God's equal and has to ask permission for everything he does.

<span class="term-study">Study: </span>[Job](/studies/the-book-of-job/)

### ḥātar
<span class="term-lang">Hebrew · to dig through</span>

Used only eight times in the Hebrew Bible, mostly for digging through walls. In Jonah 1:13 the sailors “row hard,” literally *dig* through the water. In Amos 9:2 people try to dig into Sheol to escape God.

<span class="term-study">Study: </span>[Jonah](/studies/the-book-of-jonah/)

### ḥūs
<span class="term-lang">Hebrew · to have pity, to spare</span>

God uses this word for Jonah's feelings about the plant, and the very same word for His own compassion for Nineveh (Jonah 4:10–11).

<span class="term-study">Study: </span>[Jonah](/studies/the-book-of-jonah/)

### kelayot
<span class="term-lang">Hebrew · kidneys</span>

In the ancient Near East the kidneys stood for a person's innermost being. “He splits my kidneys apart” (Job 16:13); “my kidneys are consumed with longing” (Job 19:27, literally).

<span class="term-study">Study: </span>[Job](/studies/the-book-of-job/)

### kopher
<span class="term-lang">Hebrew · ransom</span>

The price paid to set someone free. Elihu speaks of a mediator who says: “I have found a ransom” (Job 33:24).

<span class="term-study">Study: </span>[Job](/studies/the-book-of-job/)

### Leviathan
<span class="term-lang">Hebrew · “the twisting one”</span>

The great sea monster that stands for chaos (Job 3:8; Job 41; Psalm 74:14; Psalm 104:26; Isaiah 27:1). Only God can control it.

<span class="term-study">Studies: </span>[Job](/studies/the-book-of-job/) · [Jonah](/studies/the-book-of-jonah/)

### mal'ak
<span class="term-lang">Hebrew · messenger, angel</span>

The envoy Elihu speaks about in Job 33:23, “one among a thousand.”

<span class="term-study">Study: </span>[Job](/studies/the-book-of-job/)

### melits
<span class="term-lang">Hebrew · interpreter, mediator</span>

Someone who speaks on behalf of another, like a translator or ambassador (Job 33:23).

<span class="term-study">Study: </span>[Job](/studies/the-book-of-job/)

### metsulah
<span class="term-lang">Hebrew · the depths</span>

The deep, deadly sea: where Jonah is thrown (Jonah 2:3), where Leviathan makes the water boil (Job 41:31), where Pharaoh's army sank (Exodus 15:5), and where God throws our sins (Micah 7:19).

<span class="term-study">Studies: </span>[Jonah](/studies/the-book-of-jonah/) · [Job](/studies/the-book-of-job/)

### nabat
<span class="term-lang">Hebrew · to look intently</span>

Not a quick glance, but a focused gaze. Lot's wife looked back (Genesis 19:26); Abraham looked up at the stars (Genesis 15:5); whoever looked at the bronze serpent lived (Numbers 21:9).

<span class="term-study">Study: </span>[Don't Look Back](/studies/dont-look-back/)

### naḥash
<span class="term-lang">Hebrew · serpent</span>

In Amos 9:3 God commands the serpent at the bottom of the sea. The Greek Septuagint translates it as *drakōn*, dragon.

<span class="term-study">Study: </span>[Jonah](/studies/the-book-of-jonah/)

### nehpachet
<span class="term-lang">Hebrew · overthrown, turned around</span>

Jonah's message: “In forty days Nineveh will be *overthrown*.” The same verb is used for Sodom (Genesis 19:25), but it can also mean “turned around.” Nineveh was turned around, just not the way Jonah expected.

<span class="term-study">Study: </span>[Jonah](/studies/the-book-of-jonah/)

### qiqayon
<span class="term-lang">Hebrew · castor oil plant</span>

The fast-growing plant that gave Jonah shade, and then withered in one night (Jonah 4:6–7).

<span class="term-study">Study: </span>[Jonah](/studies/the-book-of-jonah/)

### ra'ah
<span class="term-lang">Hebrew · evil, disaster</span>

A thread through the book of Jonah: Nineveh's evil (1:2), the disaster God relents of (3:10), and finally “it was a great evil to Jonah” (4:1).

<span class="term-study">Study: </span>[Jonah](/studies/the-book-of-jonah/)

### Resheph
<span class="term-lang">Hebrew · the Canaanite god of plague</span>

Job 5:7 literally says “as the sons of Resheph fly upward,” usually translated “as the sparks fly upward.”

<span class="term-study">Study: </span>[Job](/studies/the-book-of-job/)

### Sheol
<span class="term-lang">Hebrew · the realm of the dead</span>

“Out of the belly of Sheol I cried” (Jonah 2:2).

<span class="term-study">Study: </span>[Jonah](/studies/the-book-of-jonah/)

### yarad
<span class="term-lang">Hebrew · to go down</span>

Jonah goes down to Joppa, down into the ship, down into the hold, down to the roots of the mountains (Jonah 1:3, 5; 2:6). Then God brings him up.

<span class="term-study">Study: </span>[Jonah](/studies/the-book-of-jonah/)

## Greek

### drakōn
<span class="term-lang">Greek · dragon</span>

The Septuagint's word for the serpent in Amos 9:3, fitting the image of a sea monster like Leviathan.

<span class="term-study">Study: </span>[Jonah](/studies/the-book-of-jonah/)

### kētos
<span class="term-lang">Greek · sea monster</span>

The word for Jonah's “great fish” in the Septuagint and in Matthew 12:40. For Greek readers it meant a chaos monster, not just a big fish.

<span class="term-study">Study: </span>[Jonah](/studies/the-book-of-jonah/)
